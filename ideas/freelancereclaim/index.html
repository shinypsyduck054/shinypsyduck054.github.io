$19 AI kit for freelancers stiffed by clients: state-law-cited final demand letter invoking Freelance Isn't Free Act (NY, OR, IL, WA, NJ, CO equivalents), small claims court filing checklist, state labor board complaint template, escalation letter if demand is ignored. Differentiator: most freelancers don't know state statutes mandate $250/violation penalty + attorney fees. The threat alone often closes the gap. Pitched June 3, 2026.

R&D · ONE-TIME · 57M FREELANCER TAM · LEGAL STATUTE HOOK
THE GAP

Bonsai/HoneyBook/Wave are CRMs with invoice reminders — none generate state-law-cited demand letters. ClaimsMaximizer does not cover freelancer non-payment. DoNotPay covered debt collection but is effectively dead. LawDepot has generic templates, not AI, not freelance-specific.

No tool was found that knows about the Freelance Isn't Free Act or state-specific freelancer protection statutes. These laws create automatic penalties ($250/violation) and mandatory attorney fee awards that make small-dollar non-payment cases economically viable to pursue — but only if you know to cite them.

57M US freelancers × ~20% encounter non-payment = ~11M potential customers annually. r/freelance (450K) has constant "client won't pay" threads where every commenter gives the same generic advice instead of citing the statute that would actually work.

THE PRODUCT
  • Demand letter: State-law-cited, invokes Freelance Isn't Free Act and state equivalents where applicable, cites $250/violation penalty + attorney fees
  • Small claims checklist: State-specific, for amounts under $10K
  • Labor board complaint template: Where state labor boards have jurisdiction over freelance non-payment
  • Escalation letter: Second letter if the demand is ignored — with increased urgency
THE VERDICT
R&D · STATUTE KNOWLEDGE IS THE MOAT

The legal hook is the differentiator. Generic "send a demand letter" advice is everywhere free. A demand letter that cites the exact statute mandating $250/violation + attorney fee awards is something fundamentally different — and something that often results in immediate payment without court action.

Customer is a business owner asserting a contract right — not in life crisis. Same profile as ContractorClaim which validated the format (before ContractorClaim was killed on legal risk). FreelanceReclaim's legal basis is cleaner: documented federal and state freelancer protection statutes, not homeowner-contractor tort law.
