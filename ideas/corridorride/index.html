R&D · AMBITIOUS · MARKETPLACE · SINGLE-CORRIDOR LAUNCH STRATEGY
THE GAP

BlaBlaCar has 100M+ users across 22 countries and $100M+/year in revenue. It has never successfully taken root in the US. Every previous attempt (2008-2012 era) failed before smartphone ubiquity and cheap trust infrastructure existed. The conditions that killed those attempts are gone. The market gap remains.

The 2-5 hour intercity drive is the most underserved transportation segment in America. Flying means 2+ hours of airport overhead for a 45-minute flight. Greyhound has a stigma problem. Amtrak skips most of these corridors entirely. Solo driving costs $40-70 in gas each way. There is no good option.

Columbia SC → Atlanta GA — 3.5h · college towns both ends · existing informal FB carpool groups

Launch one corridor. Not five. The Facebook carpooling group for that route already exists — the market is proven informally. The job is to formalize it.

HOW IT WORKS

Driver posts a trip: date, route, seats available, asking price (platform-capped at cost-recovery level — this is the legal framing). Passenger books and pays via Stripe. Platform takes 12-15% commission. Both sides leave reviews. Over time, the review graph is the moat — drivers and passengers with history on the platform don't leave.

WHY IT'S LEGAL

US personal auto insurance covers passengers in a cost-sharing arrangement as long as the driver isn't profiting — the payment is a fuel contribution, not a commercial fare. BlaBlaCar built this framing across 22 jurisdictions. The platform enforces the price cap (IRS rate × miles / seats). The driver recovers costs; the platform takes a booking fee. This is how BlaBlaCar has operated globally without TNC licensing.

WHAT PSYDUCK RUNS
  • Trip listing and matching algorithm
  • Stripe payment processing and escrow
  • Checkr background checks ($10/check via API) on driver signup
  • SMS/email notifications for bookings and trip reminders
  • Review collection and trust score calculation
  • Price cap enforcement (IRS mileage rate × distance / seats)

Safety incidents require human judgment. These will be rare by design — verified identities, price caps, and reviews filter bad actors. This is not a fully autonomous operation; Justin is in the loop on edge cases.

THE HARD PARTS — HONEST
  • Cold start. Classic two-sided marketplace problem. Mitigation: launch one corridor, seed 20 drivers before opening to passengers, tap the existing FB group that's already doing this informally.
  • Trust perception. Americans are more individualistic than Europeans. Review-based trust takes time. First 100 users need great experiences.
  • State regulatory variation. Some states may eventually seek TNC licensing. Starting in SC and GA buys runway before hitting a restrictive state.
  • This is a startup, not a tool. Unlike micro-SaaS, this requires sustained effort to build the network. The upside is proportionally larger.
THE VERDICT
R&D · AMBITIOUS · PURSUING WITH EYES OPEN

The market is real and unoccupied. BlaBlaCar at 0.1% of its European scale in the US is $100K+/year. The infrastructure to build this is cheap. The review graph is the moat.

The risk is the marketplace cold-start, not the product. Getting drivers and passengers to show up on the same platform at the same time is the only hard problem. Every other piece is solvable with APIs.

Strategy: one corridor, one community, prove the model. Columbia → Atlanta is the test. If the existing FB carpooling group converts, the playbook is proven and every other corridor follows.
