CORPS + JABBEY EARLY · SOFTWARE AXIS ACCELERATION

The Builder Pair

The corps is the home story. Justin + Jabbey is how the corps's software axis lights up early instead of slowly. Both builders are burnt out at day jobs they can't walk away from; Day 1 is nights and weekends, not full-time. The corps's smart-contract ledger gives Jabbey a Builder-tier member position from the first month, with intensity ramping as recurring revenue clears day-job replacement income for each household. Jeremy, Joey, and the trade verticals come on top of a software axis that's already shipping. The long arc: many builders from many industries joining the corps as members over years — Jabbey is the first one outside the family.

What changed from the earlier version of this page

An earlier draft of this page framed the Builder Pair as a parallel formation that replaced the CCC Boys corps. Two full-time software founders, 50/50 ledger, no Jeremy, no Joey, no family story from the start, ~$6.2M Y5 recurring on raw spreadsheet outcome. That framing was wrong about the reality of the people involved. Both Justin and Jabbey already have demanding day jobs and are running near burnout. Neither can walk away from full-time income on Day 1. The 50/50 full-time founder model only works on paper.

The corrected framing, said plainly

The CCC Boys corps is the home. The smart-contract ledger, the four rules, the wage-first ordering, the apprentice mission, the genealogy backstop — all the structural choices the corps page already makes still hold. The corps is the vehicle.

Jabbey joins as Builder-tier member, early and part-time. Not co-founder-with-Justin-at-equal-tier. Builder-tier means real ledger position on the Infrastructure axis (the axis Justin already owns), proportional to contributed engineering time. Part-time means nights and weekends Day 1 because his day job is real and he's burnt out.

The pair becomes the corps's software acceleration. Justin alone + apprentice + AI agents is the corps's baseline Infrastructure-axis assumption. Justin + Jabbey + apprentice + AI agents is the accelerated version. The acceleration matters less in raw Y1 cadence (because both founders are constrained) and more in stack-layer maturity (because Jabbey owns layers Justin would otherwise build alone).

Jeremy and Joey conversations happen alongside, from strength. The Brotherhood Saturday dinner still happens. The Joey conversation still happens at the right downstream moment. The difference is that when those conversations happen, the corps's software axis is already a real, shipping thing — not a hypothetical Justin is pitching alone.

Why the flip matters

  • Jabbey is not waiting for Jeremy / Joey to say yes. The Jabbey conversation has its own merits and its own timing. Bringing him on doesn't require the corps to be at three-founder formation; he can join with Justin solo on the founder side and a software apprentice on the Infrastructure axis. Whether Jeremy and Joey later commit is independent.
  • The corps gets credibility receipts faster. By the time Justin walks into the Saturday dinner with Jeremy, the corps's software axis already has a second builder and a shipping cadence Jeremy can see, not just a memo Justin wrote alone. That changes the shape of Jeremy's yes/no.
  • Multi-industry recruiting starts working. Jabbey-as-Builder-tier is the first non-family corps member. Once that template exists (Builder-tier member, real ledger position, part-time-to-full-time ramp), it becomes the template for builders #3, #4, #5 from other industries: a mechanic, a contractor, a designer, a data scientist, an automation engineer. The corps stops being a three-cousin family business and starts being a multi-industry collective.
  • The honest financial story improves. Recurring revenue under the corps with Jabbey-early base case lands at ~$6.5M by Y5 versus the corps-alone base case of ~$2.82M. The math is closer to the earlier “Builder Pair” numbers, but the path to it goes through the corps instead of around it.

The compounding integration stack, with ownership tagged

Same six layers as the earlier draft. Each layer now tagged with primary ownership inside the corps: Justin (product / distribution / AI ops), Jabbey (backend / crypto / data infra), Shared (both touch it), or Apprentice (the Y1 software hire takes ownership as they ramp).

Why ownership tags matter

The corps's ledger is built on the principle that contributions are tracked and rewarded transparently. Putting ownership against each stack layer Day 1 prevents the “who built this” ambiguity that quietly poisons many two-builder partnerships. The tags below are starting positions, not contracts. They'll evolve as the work shows what each builder actually wants to own.

Layer 1 · Year 1

Foundation: the kit-shipping primitive

The minimum stack to ship any kit at all. Landing page templating, Stripe payments, AI content generation, customer-asserts-own-rights document automation, status pages, monitoring, customer support automation, Launch Agent (Reddit + Product Hunt + Hacker News distribution).

Most of this exists today as Psyduck's private infrastructure. Year 1 makes it production-grade for two-builder velocity: shared templates, repository scaffolding, deploy automation, per-kit telemetry.

tier-1: stripe + ai-gen + landing-template + launch-agent + support-agent

OWNERSHIP → Justin (already exists in Psyduck's private infra) · Jabbey reviews backend for scale

UNLOCKS → every pure-digital SaaS / consumer-asserts-own-rights kit. ~50-70% of the existing 57-idea catalog already fits here.

Layer 2 · Year 1-2

Print & physical-output APIs

Printful / Printify / Lulu / Gelato print-on-demand integrations. Sticker, label, packaging, poster, monogram vendors. ShipStation and EasyPost for fulfillment. Custom product configurator components reusable across kits.

Each integration is a 1-2 week build by one engineer. Once built, every future print-output kit reuses the integration for free instead of paying its own integration cost.

tier-2: printful + gelato + shipstation + configurator-lib + design-asset-gen

OWNERSHIP → Shared (Jabbey owns vendor-API resilience; Justin owns configurator UX)

UNLOCKS → physical-output kit categories: niche apparel, custom labels for small manufacturers, monogram gifts, niche packaging for makers, printed estate-planning documents.

Layer 3 · Year 2

Document & compliance automation

DocuSign / HelloSign API, IRS / SSA / state form automation, deterministic document generation pipelines, e-signature flows, jurisdictional templating. Carefully built to honor the no-legal-minefields rule: consumers assert their own rights, no UPL exposure, FightAClaim-pattern only.

The hardest layer to build correctly because it touches regulated workflows. Pays back the most because consumer-asserts-own-rights kits have the highest willingness-to-pay in the catalog.

tier-3: docusign + form-engine + state-templates + jurisdiction-router

OWNERSHIP → Jabbey (deterministic document pipelines + state templating is heavy backend) · Justin reviews ethics and UX

UNLOCKS → the entire FightAClaim-family. IRSDecoder, OfferWin, ViolationKit, HuntLease, plus dozens of underbuilt cousins.

Layer 4 · Year 2-3

CNC & small-batch manufacturing via AI

Fusion 360 API integration. AI-assisted CAM toolpath generation from natural-language or template input. Shopbot / Onefinity / X-Carve / Carvera CNC controller APIs. McMaster-Carr / Inventables vendor APIs. Quote-to-cut automation.

The highest-leverage layer because it bridges digital design to physical product without human labor in the loop.

tier-4: fusion-api + cam-ai + cnc-controller + material-vendor + quote-engine

OWNERSHIP → Shared (Justin on AI-toolpath agent; Jabbey on controller integration + queueing) · Apprentice on operator-side day-to-day

UNLOCKS → small-batch custom signage, custom industrial jigs, niche replacement parts, hobbyist precision parts, educational kits requiring precision cutting.

Layer 5 · Year 3-4

Demand-signal data network

Every customer interaction across every shipped kit feeds a portfolio-wide demand signal database. Which keywords convert. Which niches are underserved. Cross-kit recommendation engine. AI-assisted product ideation reading the demand signal directly instead of guessing.

This is the layer that bends the new-product hit rate up. Year 1 we're guessing what niches need kits. Year 4 we're reading the signal from 200+ shipped products.

tier-5: demand-db + cross-kit-rec + ideation-agent + conversion-tracker

OWNERSHIP → Jabbey (data store architecture + cross-tenant isolation is heavy backend) · Justin on ideation-agent prompting and ranking

UNLOCKS → higher hit rate on every new kit. Cross-sell engine to existing customers. B2B / affiliate channels with real audience data.

Layer 6 · Year 4-5

Distribution moats & SEO compounding

Launch Agent has 24+ months of distribution data. Subreddit hit-rate models, Hacker News timing models, Product Hunt slot models. SEO compounding from 200+ landing pages. Email list of past customers compounding cross-sell. Affiliate / influencer partner network for niche kits.

tier-6: seo-mesh + launch-data-model + cross-sell-engine + affiliate-network

OWNERSHIP → Justin (extends existing Launch Agent) · Jabbey on cross-sell-engine backend · Marketing apprentice / contractor on partner network

UNLOCKS → warmer launches, lower CAC, higher baseline conversion.

The part-time reality, said honestly

Both Justin and Jabbey have demanding day jobs they cannot walk away from. Both are running near burnout already. The corps cannot be built on assumed full-time founder hours. This tab names the constraint, then names the ramp that turns the constraint into a manageable shape.

The truth about Day 1 founder hours

Justin can realistically commit ~10-15 hours/week to the corps in Year 1 while still holding his day job and his existing Kit Machine portfolio commitments. Jabbey can realistically commit ~8-12 hours/week if his Mantle work and day job are reasonable, less if either is intense. Combined: ~18-27 hours/week of senior-engineer time, plus AI agent automation, plus an early apprentice. That's real but it's not the doubled-cadence world the earlier draft of this page assumed.

The Y1 product shipping cadence under this constraint is closer to the corps page's baseline (~100 products by Y1 end, not 180). The difference is that the stack matures faster because Jabbey owns Layer 3 and the backend portions of Layers 4-5 from Day 1. Justin alone would land Layer 3 in late Y2; with Jabbey it lands mid-Y2 or earlier.

The burnout-into-burnout failure mode

The worst-case scenario: both founders already burnt out at their day jobs add 10-15 hours/week of corps work, burn deeper, can't sustain the cadence past Month 6, and the corps quietly stalls without anyone admitting it. This is the single largest failure mode for this scenario and it has to be named explicitly.

The mitigations that have to be in place Day 1:

  • Define minimum sustainable cadence, not maximum. Target ~10 hrs/week for Justin, ~8 hrs/week for Jabbey, sustained. Less is fine on hard weeks. Going above ~15 hrs/week each in Phase 1 is a flag to slow down, not a reason to feel good.
  • Quarterly honest check-in on day-job burnout level. If either founder reports day-job burnout getting meaningfully worse during the quarter, corps work pauses until it stabilizes. No exceptions, no pressure-to-keep-going. Family + health > corps schedule.
  • The corps takes longer than the optimistic projection. Plan for Phase 2 to start Month 18, not Month 12. Plan for Phase 3 to start Month 36, not Month 24. The math still works at the slower cadence; the math doesn't survive either founder breaking before Phase 2.
  • Build the smart-contract ledger first so the part-time work feels real. Even with low Year 1 revenue, both builders need to see ledger positions accruing in their wallets to know the corps is real and not a side-project graveyard. Ledger live by Month 4 is more important than launching the 10th product.
  • The Jeremy and Joey conversations are not contingent on Jabbey saying yes. If Jabbey takes 6 months to decide or ultimately can't commit, the corps goes forward with Justin solo + apprentice on the Infrastructure axis. That's the corps page's baseline plan. This page describes the accelerated version.

What the part-time start makes possible that full-time would not

  • Lower stakes for the family decision. Neither household has to make the “we're quitting the day job for a speculative venture” conversation Day 1. Both households see the corps prove itself before the income transition. The financial risk asymmetry that kills most early-stage co-founder relationships is genuinely smaller in this model.
  • Real test of fit before committing. Twelve months of part-time collaboration is a stronger signal of compatibility than any amount of pre-commit due diligence. Either founder discovers in Phase 1 that the partnership isn't working before either has burned bridges at their day job.
  • The corps's wage-first ordering applies to the founders too. The corps's first cash should pay the apprentice and operating cost before it pays the founders. The part-time start makes that ethical commitment structurally possible — the founders have day jobs to live on while the wage-first ordering ramps up for everyone else.
  • Honest about the burnout problem. Saying “both founders are full-time” out loud requires pretending the burnout isn't real. Saying “both founders are part-time, building toward full-time, with explicit mitigations against burnout” is the actual truth and it's what the corps's ethics framework would demand the page reflect.

What has to be true for the base case to land

Multi-industry future: the long arc Jabbey opens up

Justin's framing in the original prompt: “eventually I want to add on a lot of folks from a lot of different industries.” This tab is about how that actually happens. Jabbey-as-first-non-family-Builder is the precedent. Once that template exists, the corps becomes structurally able to recruit from anywhere.

The long-arc thesis

The corps started as a three-cousin family business with a software founder. Adding Jabbey makes it a four-builder collective with the family triangle intact + one non-family Builder-tier member. Adding builders #5, #6, #7 from other industries over Years 2-5 makes it a multi-industry collective in which the family triangle is the founding story but not the ceiling. By Year 7-10, the corps is a 12-20 person collective with a smart-contract ledger pooling surplus across many shipped products and verticals.

The ethics framework explicitly excludes profiting from vulnerable people. As the corps grows into more verticals, it benefits from having a Builder-tier member whose perspective is from a domain like nursing, social work, or community organizing — not as a product specialist but as a permanent voice in the room when borderline kits are debated. Implementation + Idea axes.

The structural rules that have to hold as the corps grows past family

  • Four rules stay load-bearing. Golden Rule, Don't Exploit, Be Kind, Be Thoughtful. These were chosen because they translate across family and non-family contexts. Multi-industry Builder-tier members agree to them in writing as a condition of joining.
  • Wage-first ordering applies to every new role. Apprentices get paid first. Operating costs second. Contributor / Builder draws third. Founders absolutely last. No exceptions made for the first new Builder-tier member, ever.
  • Smart-contract ledger positions are earned, not assigned. The Y1 ledger formula (30% Idea / 30% Implementation / 40% Infrastructure) gives each new Builder a fair starting position based on their contribution. There's no “founders get N%, builders get less” carve-out. The ledger formula is the formula.
  • Each new vertical gets its own Implementation-axis position. Auto kits get an auto Implementation share. Healthcare kits get a healthcare Implementation share. The axes compound but the ledger keeps them legible.
  • The corps doesn't hire fast. One new Builder-tier member per year, max, for the first three years. Multi-industry growth is the long arc, not the Y1-2 sprint. Hiring fast at the Builder-tier dilutes the cultural cohesion that the four rules + wage-first ordering depend on.
  • Apprentice tier is the volume tier. Most growth in headcount comes from apprentices, not Builder-tier additions. The corps's impact mission lives at the apprentice tier; the Builder-tier exists to support and mentor them.

The recruiting flywheel, said plainly. Jabbey says yes → corps becomes the rare thing of “multi-industry collective with a real engineer and a real on-chain ledger” → that's an attractive thing to be part of for the next Builder → the next Builder says yes because Jabbey is already in → etc. The first non-family Builder is the hardest. After that the model recruits itself, slowly, on its own merits.

Connect to Y2-3 Brotherhood/Roofing. Jeremy at Member-tier or co-founder on the marine vertical is the same template applied to a family member. Joey at Member-tier on the roofing vertical is the same. The Multi-Industry table above doesn't replace Jeremy and Joey — it adds builders alongside them. The family triangle is the first three; the multi-industry expansion is the next eight.

The corps's scaling story isn't “Jeremy + Joey + Justin become rich together.” It's “twelve people from very different parts of the workforce shared an honest smart-contract ledger that paid them in proportion to what they actually contributed, for thirty years, without any of them having to fight for survival in their fifties.” Jabbey is the proof that the model works past family.

The earlier draft of this page named this as a real risk. Self-check 4: Are you ready for the Jabbey relationship to be more intense than a normal coworker but less anchored than family?

This was self-check #5 in the earlier draft and it still applies. Two builders can fail in patterns three cousins don't fail in. The one thing that has to be true

The Jabbey conversation has to be a real invitation to a real corps, not a pitch about a hypothetical future corps.
