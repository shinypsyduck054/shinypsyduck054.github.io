THE GAP

The solar industry has a trust problem baked into its largest consumer platform. EnergySage — the go-to comparison site — earns referral commissions from installers. Their incentive is for you to sign, not for you to get the best deal. They structurally cannot tell a homeowner "this quote is overpriced."

Every other "solar proposal software" product is B2B installer tooling (Aurora Solar, Energy Toolbase, Scoop Solar) — built to help installers create proposals, not to help homeowners evaluate them. Zero consumer-facing proposal reviewers exist.

Meanwhile, r/solar (320K members) has a constant stream of "is this quote fair?" posts — homeowners manually searching NREL data, asking strangers to sanity-check $/watt figures, trying to understand production estimates on their own. This is the unmet need made visible.

Macro tailwind: electricity costs are only going up. Grid stress, EV adoption, data center demand — the ROI case for solar improves every year. More homeowners will get quotes. More homeowners will want a gut-check before committing $15–30K.

THE PRODUCT

Homeowner pastes or uploads their solar proposal. SolarVet extracts the key variables — system size (kW), panel brand and tier, inverter specs, $/watt price, production estimate (kWh/year), finance terms — and benchmarks each one against:

NREL PVWatts API — free production estimate by zip code; flags if installer's estimate is inflated
SEIA regional pricing data — national and regional $/watt averages by system size
EnergySage public averages — their published national benchmarks (the irony: their own data becomes the tool that checks against them)

Output: plain-English grade (A–F), red flag list with explanations ("your $/watt is 18% above regional average"), and a list of specific questions to ask the installer before signing.

Pricing: $19 one-time per proposal review. $29 multi-quote comparison (upload 2–3 quotes, get a ranked comparison). No subscription.

Not regulated advice. We compare specs and pricing against public benchmarks — the same thing Wirecutter and Consumer Reports do for every other product category. We don't tell them whether to go solar, we tell them whether this quote is in line with the market.

COMPETITOR SWEEP

Both targeted searches ("consumer solar quote reviewer AI SaaS" and "solar proposal analysis tool homeowner") returned zero purpose-built competitors.

  • EnergySage — lead-gen marketplace. Structurally misaligned: earns commission when you sign. Cannot tell you the quote is bad.
  • Aurora Solar / Energy Toolbase / Scoop Solar — B2B installer proposal creation tools. Different market entirely.
  • Generic AI chat (ChatGPT, Claude) — will answer questions but has no structured proposal parsing, no live NREL/SEIA data, and no UX tailored to this workflow.
  • r/solar community — free but slow, inconsistent, and requires a homeowner to know what questions to ask.

Gap confidence: HIGH. No ProductHunt hit, no IndieHackers hit, no Reddit discussion of an existing tool. The consumer side of solar quote analysis is completely unoccupied.

DISTRIBUTION

First dollar path: one post on r/solar ("I built a tool that benchmarks solar quotes against NREL and SEIA data — is this useful?"). The community literally does this manually every day. SEO follows: "is my solar quote fair," "solar quote too expensive," "$/watt solar quote calculator," "[state] solar average cost 2026" — all high-intent keywords currently served only by EnergySage and generic blog posts. Every EnergySage result is a competitor with misaligned incentives; we are the search result that actually helps.

RISKS — HONEST
STATE INCENTIVE DATA UPKEEP — Federal ITC is stable, but state-level incentives (rebates, net metering rules, SREC programs) change frequently. We flag this in the output but keeping the data current is ongoing work. Manageable, not trivial.
PROPOSAL FORMAT VARIABILITY — Every installer has their own proposal PDF format. Parsing structured data from unstructured proposals requires robust extraction logic. First version should accept manual input (user types in the key numbers) before going full PDF-parse.
LOW-PROBABILITY REGULATORY RISK — If EnergySage or installer lobbies ever pushed a "certified solar advisor" licensing scheme, this could get complicated. Extremely unlikely — we're Consumer Reports for solar quotes, not a licensed energy consultant. But worth knowing the risk exists.
THE VERDICT
R&D · SOLID GAP · GROWING TAM

The conflict of interest in the market is structural and unfixable. EnergySage cannot tell a homeowner their quote is bad — that's not a feature gap, it's a business model constraint. SolarVet is structurally free of that conflict. That's the core differentiator, and it's one a well-funded competitor can't easily copy.

The TAM grows automatically. Electricity costs are on a one-way trend. Every rate hike makes solar more compelling, puts more homeowners in the quote-evaluation funnel, and increases the urgency of getting that evaluation right. We don't have to manufacture demand — it's growing on its own.

The main drag is ongoing data upkeep. State incentives change. That's a real cost, not a blocker. Build v1 with federal-only benchmarking and add state layers progressively. The NREL and SEIA data are free and stable. Start there.
