PIPKIT

PIP survival toolkit for employees placed on Performance Improvement Plans. Turned down: most PIPs end in termination regardless of effort. The ethics filter killed it before the market research did. Pitched and passed May 30, 2026.

PASS · ETHICS FILTER FAILED
THE ORIGINAL PITCH

Employees placed on PIPs are scared, confused, and searching for answers. Most don't know their rights, don't know how to document their performance, and don't know when a PIP is pretextual (already decided) vs. genuine. A survival kit — response templates, documentation checklists, legal rights explainer, how to read between the lines — could give them a fighting chance for $29.

The demand signal is obvious. r/antiwork and r/cscareerquestions run "I got a PIP, what do I do?" threads every week. Search volume is real. Pain is high. Motivation to pay is high.

It didn't matter. The idea didn't survive the ethics filter.

WHY IT FAILED — ETHICS

The hard truth about PIPs: most of them end in termination. HR research consistently shows that a majority of PIP recipients are let go within 90 days — often because the PIP was never genuinely intended as a correction path. It's a paper trail for a decision that's already been made.

Selling a $29 kit to someone in that position means taking money from a person who is statistically about to lose their job. They're not buying a solution — they're buying hope that probably won't pan out. That's the wrong side of the equation.

The filter: revenue should flow toward victims, not from them. The PIP kit customer is the person being hurt by a system designed to fail them. That's MedSettle all over again — different problem, same dynamic.

THE KILL DECISION
PASS · ETHICS FILTER

Customer and victim are the same person. A PIP is, in the majority of cases, a managed exit disguised as a performance process. The person searching for "how to survive a PIP" is often searching for a way out of a situation that has already been decided against them.

Taking money from people about to lose their jobs doesn't feel right — and it isn't. The demand is real. The market gap may be real. But the ethics aren't there. We don't build businesses where the paying customer is also the person the broken system is designed to hurt.

Same reasoning that killed MedSettle. Consistent filter. Easy pass.

WHAT WOULD PASS THE FILTER

A free resource — genuinely free, not freemium — funded by something else (employment attorneys who get referrals from people who actually do have wrongful termination cases) might work. But that's a legal referral business, not a PIP kit. Different idea entirely.
