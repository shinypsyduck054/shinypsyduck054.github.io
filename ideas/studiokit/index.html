AI studio-policy kit for private music teachers (and the broader category: art, tutoring, dance, martial arts, fitness, “insert here”). The kit pattern looked clean. The documents inside it turned out to be legal contracts. Passed June 17, 2026.

PASS · LEGAL MINEFIELD · UPL EXPOSURE · NOT A CRON-DISQUALIFIER (YET)
THE PITCH (BEFORE THE BREAKDOWN)

$29/$49 kit for private music teachers (and adjacent verticals): generates a complete studio policy plus enrollment contract, recital media release, summer gap agreement, and late-payment notice. Customized to the teacher's rules by AI in 5 minutes. r/pianolessons (250K) full of teachers asking each other “how do I handle missed lessons,” existing studio-management SaaS proves WTP. Distribution channel obvious. First-dollar path tight. The pattern looked indistinguishable from FightAClaim or HuntLease at a glance.

THE BREAKDOWN, BY DOCUMENT

Three of five documents in the pitched kit are unambiguously legal contracts. The fourth is a quasi-contract; the fifth has its own regulatory exposure.

DocumentLegal nature
Studio PolicyQuasi-contract (terms of service). Edge case.
Enrollment ContractContract. Binding agreement between studio and parent.
Recital Media ReleaseContract. Waiver of likeness rights.
Summer Gap AgreementContract. Retainer/hold agreement.
Late Payment NoticeCollections letter. UDAP/FDCPA-adjacent.

The value prop in the pitch was literally “generates your complete studio policy customized to your rules in 5 minutes.” That phrase — AI-personalized legal documents for non-lawyer buyers — is the textbook UPL (Unauthorized Practice of Law) exposure. The AI doing the personalization makes it worse, not better, because personalization looks like legal advice to a state bar regulator.

WHY WE PASSED
UPL EXPOSURE PROFILE — The kit generates binding contracts (enrollment, release, retainer) personalized by AI for non-lawyer users to issue to a third party (the parent/student). That is the highest-exposure shape in the legal-doc generator space. LegalZoom and Rocket Lawyer have spent millions defending UPL suits over exactly this pattern. State bar regulators interpret AI personalization of contracts as practicing law without a license in the lay user's jurisdiction.
CONTRAST WITH FIGHTACLAIM — FightAClaim and the existing kit portfolio sit on safer UPL ground because they generate demand letters (the customer asserts their own rights against a counterparty) and decision-support content (the customer learns rules and acts). StudioKit generates contracts with a counterparty. Different exposure category. FightAClaim survives the test; StudioKit doesn't.
BROADER CATEGORY IS SAME PROBLEM — The pattern (“policy kit for private music / art / tutoring / dance / martial arts / fitness teachers”) doesn't change the exposure. Enrollment contracts and liability releases live in every one of those verticals. Every “insert here” substitution carries the same UPL profile.
DISCLAIMERS DON'T FIX IT — “Not legal advice” boilerplate is necessary but not sufficient. LegalZoom and Rocket Lawyer both have heavy disclaimers and both have been sued (and settled) on UPL grounds in multiple states. Disclaimers reduce damages but don't eliminate the action.
SAFER VERSION EXISTS BUT IS A DIFFERENT (WEAKER) PRODUCT — A non-contract version is possible: studio policy (terms of service, less risky) + workflow templates (attendance, billing) + communication scripts (parent emails, payment reminders). Strips out the contracts, releases, and retainers. The product survives UPL but loses the differentiated value. Not enough left to charge $29 for.
DAILY CRON HAD NO UPL DISQUALIFIER — The business-idea-research cron prompt lists six disqualifiers (saturation, B2B enterprise, feature-not-product, no fast first dollar, physical presence, customer-is-victim) but does not have a UPL/legal-doc-generation rule. That's the gap that let this pitch reach the inbox. Documented separately for the cron-prompt revision.
THE LESSON

The kit pattern is not uniformly safe. There are at least three sub-shapes:

  • Demand-letter category (safer) — customer asserts their own rights against a counterparty (FightAClaim, ContractorClaim).
  • Decision-support category (safer) — tool explains rules, customer decides and acts (IRSDecoder, AnchorDoc).
  • Contract-generation category (unsafe) — AI drafts a binding agreement for the customer to issue to a third party. StudioKit lives here. UPL exposure is the highest in this shape.

Going forward: if the kit produces a contract that the customer hands to a counterparty, hard pass unless the structure provides for attorney review. The personal-trainer kit got killed by Genie AI coverage; StudioKit gets killed by UPL exposure; both should have been caught at vetting step 2, not at pitch time.

THE VERDICT
PASS · LEGAL MINEFIELD · UPL EXPOSURE

The kit generates legal contracts for non-lawyer buyers to issue to third parties. That is the textbook UPL exposure the “no legal minefields” memory rule was meant to keep us away from. AI personalization of those documents makes it worse, not better. Stripping the contracts out leaves a product too thin to justify the price. The category extension (art, tutoring, dance, fitness) doesn't escape the exposure because every one of those verticals also bundles enrollment contracts and liability releases.

Score: 2.0/10. Below CampWise because the failure mode is regulatory, not market-saturation. The market gap is real and the WTP is proven; the build can't be done safely. Cron prompt revision documented separately so the same shape doesn't reach the inbox again.
