THE GAP

Multiple western states have enacted laws protecting homeowners from HOA fines for water-efficient landscaping choices:

  • California: Water Code 714.5 prohibits HOAs from fining for replacement of lawn with drought-tolerant or water-efficient landscaping. Also prohibits fines for brown lawns during water restrictions declared by a local agency.
  • Nevada: NRS 116.2117 prohibits HOAs from requiring homeowners to install or maintain grass that the state has designated as prohibited under water conservation measures.
  • Texas: Property Code 202.007 prohibits deed restrictions from banning drought-resistant landscaping, rain barrels, and water-conserving devices.
  • Colorado: HB 15-1229 and subsequent legislation prohibit HOAs from banning xeriscaping as a primary landscaping method.
  • Arizona: ARS 33-1818 limits HOA authority over water-efficient landscaping alternatives.

Despite these laws, HOA fine letters for brown lawns and xeriscaping appear every summer in California, Nevada, and Texas HOA communities. Homeowners assume the HOA must be right and either water during restrictions or pay fines -- both of which may themselves violate local water ordinances.
