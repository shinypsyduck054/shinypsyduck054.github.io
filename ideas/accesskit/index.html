THE GAP

42 U.S.C. 3604(f)(3)(A) requires HOAs and housing providers to allow residents with disabilities to make "reasonable modifications" to a dwelling or common area at the resident's expense. This covers: wheelchair ramps, grab bars in bathrooms, accessible door hardware, wider doorways, accessible parking spaces, railing modifications, and more.

HOAs deny these requests constantly -- sometimes out of ignorance of federal law, sometimes deliberately to avoid setting precedent. The resident is often elderly, newly disabled, or caring for a disabled family member. They're navigating a new disability while simultaneously fighting their HOA. They don't know they have a federal right. They don't know HUD takes complaints. They don't know what a reasonable modification request letter looks like.

HUD's FHEO office handles these complaints, and HOAs that deny lawful requests face significant liability. But getting to a complaint requires first sending a proper request letter that documents the refusal. That's what AccessKit provides.

No AI tool generates FHA reasonable modification request letters for housing. AccommoDoc covers workplaces. This is the housing gap.

THE PRODUCT

Resident enters their disability category (mobility, vision, hearing, other), the modification needed (ramp, grab bars, parking, etc.), and HOA's response type (flat denial, unreasonable conditions, ignored request, approval with excessive cost-sharing demand) -- AI cites FHA 3604(f)(3)(A) specifically, the HUD reasonable modification guidance, and outputs a formal request letter. If HOA has already denied, the letter documents the denial for HUD complaint purposes. Standard disclaimer: this letter is for informational purposes -- for legal representation, consult an attorney.
