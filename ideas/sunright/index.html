THE GAP

26+ states have enacted solar access laws that explicitly limit HOA authority over residential solar installations. In most of these states, HOAs can only impose aesthetic requirements (placement, color, panel type) -- they cannot outright deny installation. Yet r/solar (320K members), r/HOA (170K members), and r/SolarDIY are full of posts that follow the same pattern: homeowner wants panels, HOA says no, homeowner gives up.

The statutory rights are real and well-documented. The awareness is not. Large solar installers (Sunrun, SunPower, Tesla Solar) have HOA specialists baked into their sales process -- but they're not the target customer. The target is the DIY installer, the cash buyer working with a small local shop, and the homeowner whose HOA came after them post-install when the installer relationship is long over.

No AI tool writes the state-specific demand letter. Solar installers don't provide it systematically. Legal aid organizations don't cover it. The homeowner's only option today is hire a lawyer (overkill) or give up (what most do).
