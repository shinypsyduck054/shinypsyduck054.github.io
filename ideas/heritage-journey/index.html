THE GAP

45M+ first-generation immigrants live in the United States. Their children are acutely aware that the family's origin story — why they left, what they risked, what they sacrificed to come here — lives only in the memory of aging parents and grandparents. When that generation is gone, the story is gone.

FamilyScript serves the genealogy hobbyist (Ancestry.com subscriber, focused on historical research). Heritage Journey serves a different emotional moment: the first-generation adult child who wants to capture their immigrant parent's story now, in a way the whole family can hold onto.

Reelmind.ai has a blog post referencing "1st gen immigrant story platforms" as an emerging category — but no actual product in this space was found. No standalone "guided interview → AI writes immigrant family narrative → printed hardcover" tool exists.

THE TWO-TIER PRODUCT
TIER 1 — DIGITAL KIT · $39 ONE-TIME
Child (or grandchild) answers a structured guided questionnaire: country of origin, year of arrival, reason for leaving, the journey, first years in the US, sacrifices made, what was built, messages to future generations → Claude writes a structured, emotionally resonant family narrative → formatted PDF delivered instantly via email.

Optional: interview-mode where the child reads prompts to an elder and types their responses in real-time — turns the purchase into a family conversation.
TIER 2 — HARDCOVER FAMILY BOOK · $169 (PRODIGI PRINT + DROPSHIP)
Same narrative in a premium 8×10 hardcover (50–80 pages, dust jacket, acid-free paper). Prodigi print + dropship — ships in 5–7 days.

Margin: $169 - ~$45 Prodigi cost = ~$124 profit per book.
Conversion target: 20–25% upgrade. Immigrant family gift culture (birthday gifts, holiday gifts, "something meaningful") drives upgrade motivation.
Blended LTV at 22%: ~$67 per customer.
INFRASTRUCTURE OVERLAP
Heritage Journey shares the Prodigi print pipeline with FamilyScript, AirbnbBook, Mission Trip Book, and Quinceañera Book.

Fifth product in the print family — zero additional infrastructure build cost. The AI narrative → physical book pattern continues to scale across culturally specific communities that mainstream tools ignore.

Notably, Heritage Journey and FamilyScript serve adjacent but distinct audiences: FamilyScript targets genealogy hobbyists (research-first, Ancestry.com users). Heritage Journey targets immigrant families motivated by urgency (elders are aging, story will be lost) rather than hobby interest.
COMPETITOR SWEEP
  • Reelmind.ai: Mentions "1st gen immigrant story platforms" in a blog post about AI video tools. Not a narrative book product. Not a competitor.
  • FamilyScript: Our own product — serves genealogy research compilation, not immigrant story interviews. Adjacent but distinct customer and workflow.
  • StoryWorth / Remento: Interview-based family story services — StoryWorth sends weekly questions by email, Remento uses voice-to-text. Neither is immigrant-narrative-specific, neither has an AI writing layer, neither has a print fulfillment tier. Closest indirect competitors. Need deeper vet.
  • Gap assessment: Surface sweep shows no direct AI immigrant narrative + print tool. StoryWorth/Remento may partially overlap — deeper competitive analysis required before building.
DISTRIBUTION

Communities: r/FirstGeneration (active), r/ImmigrantFamilies, culture-specific communities (r/AsianAmerican, r/latinos, r/Filipino, r/southasians — each 100K-500K members), TikTok first-gen creator space (extremely active, emotionally viral content).

SEO: "how to record my parents' immigration story", "immigrant family history book", "first generation family story" — high intent, currently served only by generic memoir-writing blog posts.

Emotional hook: "Your parents' story shouldn't die with them." TikTok content in this space consistently goes viral — first-gen creators talking about immigrant parent sacrifices regularly hits millions of views. This product is the obvious next step after watching one of those videos.

Language: English primary, but Spanish/Portuguese/Tagalog variants would unlock specific community trust. Kit Machine adds ES and PT-BR first.

REVENUE MODEL

TAM: 45M+ first-generation immigrants in the US. TAM is not annual — it's lifetime, but with recurring gift occasions (Mother's Day, Father's Day, cultural holidays, milestone birthdays).

Conservative year-one: 200 digital sales × $39 = $7,800
Book upsell at 22%: 44 books × $124 = $5,456
Year-one floor: ~$13K

At 0.1% annual penetration of a tiny slice of the TAM: $50-150K/year. The market is enormous — the question is conversion rate, which depends heavily on the emotional hook landing in the right channels.

RISKS — HONEST
  • StoryWorth/Remento overlap. These are indirect competitors that serve a partially overlapping need. Need a proper head-to-head vet before building to confirm the differentiation is real and meaningful to buyers.
  • Market breadth = diffuse distribution. "Immigrant family" is not one community — it's dozens of distinct cultures with different languages and social channels. Quinceañera Book targets one culture precisely. Heritage Journey targets everyone, which makes it harder to distribute.
  • Sensitive content. Immigration stories may include trauma, undocumented status, or painful history. The product must handle this with care — prompts should not push users toward content they aren't ready to share, and privacy is paramount.
