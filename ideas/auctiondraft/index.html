THE GAP

Every charity auction — school galas, hospital fundraisers, church benefit nights, arts organization events — needs a catalog of donated items with compelling descriptions. A well-written item description ("Dinner for 4 at Nobu, valued at $600 — an intimate evening at one of LA's most celebrated tables") drives bids. A volunteer-written one ("Dinner at restaurant - $600 value") doesn't.

The problem: PTO volunteers are writing 40–80 item descriptions at midnight before the event. Nobody wants to do it, nobody knows what format drives bids, and the result is a flat, generic PDF that leaves money on the table.

Auction platforms (CharityAuctionsToday, Auctria, OneCause, Givebutter) run the bidding. None of them generate the catalog copy. That's the gap.
