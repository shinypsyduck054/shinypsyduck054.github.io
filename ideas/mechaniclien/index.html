R&D · ONE-TIME · CONTRACTOR TAM · ENTERPRISE GAP
THE GAP

Levelset (formerly Zlien) raised $500M+ and serves medium-to-large construction companies with volume lien filing needs at $300–500+/month. Solo tradespeople — the plumber who did $8K of work and got ghosted — have literally nothing built for them. r/Contractor has a "client won't pay, what do I do?" thread every week, and the answers are always generic.

State-specific requirements vary dramatically: CA requires a 20-day preliminary notice before any lien rights attach; TX has monthly deadlines tied to the last day of the month of last work; FL gives 90 days from last work; NY requires filing a Notice of Mechanic's Lien with the county clerk. Free generic templates fail the state-specificity test — a contractor using the wrong form or missing a deadline forfeits their lien rights entirely.

Zero indie or self-serve products found for solo subcontractors needing a one-time lien kit. SEO for "how to file a mechanic's lien in [state]" returns only state bar association PDFs and Levelset's marketing content — no purpose-built indie tool exists.

THE PRODUCT
  • Notice of Intent to Lien: Required by most states before filing — if the contractor skips this step, the lien is invalid
  • Mechanic's / Construction Lien: State-specific document with correct statutory language, deadlines, and county/clerk filing instructions
  • Lien Release template: For when the client finally pays — a lien on title blocks the property from selling or refinancing, so releases matter

Price: $49 one-time per state kit. TX, CA, FL, NY at launch (~40% of US construction activity).

First dollar path: Publish a free "mechanic's lien guide for [Texas/California/Florida]" blog post → capture SEO traffic → $49 Stripe checkout → instant 3-doc PDF kit. First sale within 2–3 weeks of going live.

COMPETITOR SWEEP

Levelset: The only real player. Enterprise-focused, $300–500+/month, built for GCs managing dozens of jobs simultaneously. Solo contractor filing one lien per year doesn't exist in their product thinking.

Free template sites (eForms, LegalTemplates, PandaDoc): Generic, non-state-specific, no AI personalization, no deadline guidance. A contractor using these risks forfeiting lien rights on a technicality.

Local attorneys: $300–500/hour. A solo roofer with $6K outstanding doesn't call a lawyer — they eat the loss or ask Reddit.

Distribution: r/Contractor, r/Construction, trade Facebook groups (millions of members), SEO on "how to file a mechanic's lien in [state]."

THE VERDICT
R&D · ENTERPRISE GAP IS REAL

Clean gap, clean ethics. State mechanic's lien law is public statutory information — same framing as FreelanceReclaim and ContractorClaim. Customer is a business owner asserting a legal right to payment for work already completed. Not in housing, medical, or financial crisis.

One hesitation: TAM is narrower than the big document kits — not every job dispute goes to lien. But the people who need this REALLY need it, and $49 is trivial compared to an unpaid $5K–50K invoice. The filing deadline urgency drives a fast purchase decision.
