THE PITCH

44M US renter households. Landlords are legally required to maintain habitable conditions — heat, no mold, working plumbing — under implied warranty of habitability statutes in every state. Most tenants don't know their rights, don't know how to escalate, and give up. r/legaladvice and r/renter run daily threads: "landlord won't fix the heat, what do I do?"

TenantKit gives them the paperwork to force the issue: a state-law-cited habitability demand letter, a housing code complaint draft for the local enforcement agency, and a plain-English breakdown of rent withholding rights and repair-and-deduct options by state. $19. Instant PDF. No lawyer.

LEGAL RISK — UPL

The DoNotPay FTC precedent (2025 settlement) doesn't directly apply — it targeted "robot lawyer" false marketing, not document assembly. The real risk is unauthorized practice of law (UPL) at the state level.

  • Safe lane: User selects document type from a menu → tool fills blanks. No AI interpretation of their situation, no recommending which remedy to pursue.
  • Risky lane: AI analyzes their situation and recommends "you need a habitability letter" — that's arguably legal advice in California.
  • California (hardest state): Even auto-selecting a template based on user inputs may be "form selection" = legal advice. Requires pure form-filling UX.
  • North Carolina: Explicit safe harbor — document assembly software is not UPL by statute.
  • Mitigation: Document assembly framing, no "lawyer" language, attorney-reviewed templates, user picks the form type.

The $19 price point helps — courts treat it as software, not a legal service. LegalZoom and Nolo operate in this space at much higher prices without shutdown.

THE STRATEGY — KIT PLAYBOOK FIRST

TenantKit is not being evaluated in isolation. The decision is whether to build a kit business at all. If ContractorClaim or TenantKit converts — if SEO lands, if the $19 checkout works, if the community distribution plays out — the playbook is proven. The rest of the kit portfolio becomes low-risk replication.

Build one. Prove the model. Then crank.

THE BOARD'S VERDICT
R&D · WITH RESERVATIONS

Gap exists but is narrower than pitched. Free competitors cover parts of this space — LandlordReply is the closest match at nationwide scope. The defensible angle is citation accuracy and attorney-verified templates, not market absence.

Legal risk is manageable with the right UX decisions. Document assembly framing, no "lawyer" language, user-selects-form-type flow, attorney-reviewed templates. Not a dealbreaker — LegalZoom and Nolo navigate this at scale.

This idea is on hold pending kit playbook validation. Build one kit first. If it converts, TenantKit moves to active. If it doesn't, pass the whole category and move on.

RESERVATIONS ON RECORD
1. More free competition than originally scoped. LandlordReply, AI Lawyer, and Terms.Law are free nationwide tools. The "empty field" framing from the original pitch was inaccurate.

2. Kit fatigue. This is the third kit evaluated in this session. The playbook is repeatable — but it has to work once first before committing to a portfolio of them.

3. California UPL is a real constraint. The safest build may exclude auto-recommendation logic entirely, which limits the product experience.
